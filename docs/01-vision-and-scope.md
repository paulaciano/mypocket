# 1. Vision and Scope

## Vision

MyPocket is the backend core of a digital wallet. It lets people hold money in a platform account,
fund it from outside, send it instantly to other users and withdraw it back to an external account.
Every movement of money is recorded as an immutable, balanced accounting entry.

The product is API-only (REST plus domain events). There is no user interface.

## Users

| Actor | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | Registers, sees their wallets and history, sends money to other customers, withdraws money, closes a wallet. |
| **Administrator** | Restricts wallets (blocks debits or freezes them) and reactivates them. |
| **Payment provider** (simulated) | Notifies incoming funds (cash-in) and the outcome of withdrawals (cash-out) through signed webhooks. |
| **Fraud detector** (internal service) | Watches transfers in real time and asks for a wallet to be restricted when it detects an anomaly. |

## In scope

### Registration and authentication
A customer registers with email and password and logs in to get an access token (JWT).
Every customer operation is authenticated, and a customer can only see and operate their own wallets.

### Personal data stays in one place
The email is used only to log in. Everywhere else (transfers, history, events) customers and
wallets are identified by identifiers generated by the platform, so personal data never travels
beyond the user module.

### Wallets (multi-currency)
On registration the customer automatically gets one wallet per supported currency: **ARS** and **USD**.
Each wallet has its own balance and its own **account number**: a 10-digit number generated by the
platform that the customer shares to receive money. There is no currency conversion: money only
moves between wallets of the same currency.

### Wallet lifecycle
A wallet is `ACTIVE`, `DEBIT_BLOCKED`, `FROZEN` or `CLOSED`.
- **Debit blocked** wallets can receive money but cannot send or withdraw it.
- **Frozen** wallets can neither receive nor send money.
- Both restrictions can be applied by an administrator or by the fraud detector. Only an administrator
  can lift a restriction or change one for the other.
- **Closed** wallets are permanently disabled. Customers close their own wallets, which requires a zero
  balance and no withdrawals in progress.

### Cash-in (funding)
The payment provider notifies, through a signed webhook, that money arrived for a wallet. The wallet
is credited exactly once, even if the provider delivers the same notification several times.

### P2P transfers
A customer sends money from their wallet to another customer's wallet, identified by its account
number. Both wallets must have the same currency. The transfer is instantaneous and atomic: either both
sides move or neither does, under any level of concurrency.

### Cash-out (withdrawal)
A customer withdraws money to an external account. The system checks the available balance and the
operational limits, reserves the money immediately and answers that the withdrawal is pending. Later
the provider confirms or rejects it through a signed webhook. If it is rejected, the money returns to
the wallet.

### Operational limits
- Maximum amount per operation (transfers and withdrawals), per currency.
- Maximum total amount withdrawn per wallet per calendar day, per currency.

### Immutable history
Customers can see their wallet's movements. The history is built from ledger entries, which are never
updated or deleted. Corrections (such as a rejected withdrawal) are new entries.

### Idempotency
Every customer operation that moves money requires an `Idempotency-Key`. Retrying the same request
returns the original result without moving money again. Provider webhooks are deduplicated by the
provider's own reference.

### Real-time fraud detection
A separate service consumes transfer events and blocks debits of a wallet that sends more than a
configured number of transfers within one minute.

### Operations
The whole platform starts with one command (`docker compose up`). Technical and business metrics are
exposed to Prometheus and shown in a Grafana dashboard.

## Out of scope

- User interface.
- Currency conversion between ARS and USD.
- Real banking or card integrations: the payment provider is simulated.
- Identity verification (KYC), password recovery, refresh tokens, multi-factor authentication.
- Fees, interest, refunds of P2P transfers, scheduled or recurring payments.
- Reopening closed wallets.
- Production deployment (cloud, Kubernetes, high availability).
- Distributed tracing.
